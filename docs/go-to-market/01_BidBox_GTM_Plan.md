# BidBox Go-To-Market Plan
**Target: $50k–$100k MRR | Prepared by Malcon Ventures | July 2026**

---

## 1. The Strategic Position

Three players triangulate the market:

| | BidAmerica | Cleatus | BidBox |
|---|---|---|---|
| What it is | Listing planroom (est. 1996) | All-of-GovCon AI platform ($4M seed) | Bidding operating system |
| Scope | CA public works, discovery only | All government contracts, all industries | CA public works construction, full workflow |
| Ends where | The project listing | Generic proposal output | A submitted, competitive bid |
| Weakness | No workflow. 1,700 contractors in 30 years = listing ceiling | Too broad to build construction depth (job walks, bonds, DIR, prevailing wage, addenda, subs) | Unknown brand, one founder |

**BidBox's position: the system of action for public works bidding.** BidAmerica is a phone book. Cleatus is a Swiss army knife. BidBox is the estimator's operating system for one segment, one state, done completely.

The defensible asset is not the tech (both competitors prove crawling + LLM extraction is commoditized). It is (a) workflow depth generic tools won't build, (b) distribution owned through founder-led relationships, content, and programmatic SEO, and (c) domain credibility: the founder was the buyer.

## 2. ICP and Market Math

**Primary ICP:** California general contractors actively bidding public works, 2–25 employees, 0–2 dedicated estimators, $2M–$50M annual revenue, bidding 5–30 jobs/month across cities, counties, school districts, and special districts. The owner or chief estimator is the buyer. Pain is felt weekly: portal-hopping, missed addenda, chaotic bid days.

**Secondary (Phase 3):** mid-size GCs ($50M–$250M) with estimating departments, multi-seat, $1k–$2k/mo accounts. **Tertiary (do not sell yet, but capture):** subcontractors entering through Bid Room links.

**Market size check:** CA public agencies put out roughly 900–1,200 active projects monthly (per BidAmerica's own numbers). Active bidder universe is conservatively 3,000–5,000 GC firms plus a multiple of that in subs. Penetration required:

| Blended price | Customers for $50k MRR | Customers for $100k MRR | Penetration of ~4,000 GCs |
|---|---|---|---|
| $449/mo | 111 | 223 | 2.8% / 5.6% |
| $199/mo | 251 | 503 | 6.3% / 12.6% |

Premium pricing makes the goal reachable through founder-led sales plus organic. Cheap pricing requires a marketing machine that doesn't exist yet. Price premium.

## 3. Pricing and Packaging

Price like a hire, not like software. The comps in the buyer's head: an estimator at $8k–$12k/mo loaded, and the value of one job they didn't miss.

| Tier | Price | Contents | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tracker** | $199/mo | Discovery + calendar + deadline/addenda alerts for your coverage area | Entry ramp; makes Command look obvious |
| **Command** | $449/mo (flat per company) | Everything: document analysis, requirement extraction, qualification flags, Bid Rooms, sub coordination, Excel export | The product. 80%+ of customers land here |
| **Desk** (Phase 3) | $1,250/mo | Multi-seat, team workflows, priority ingestion of requested agencies | Upmarket expansion |

Rules:
- **Per company, not per seat.** Removes adoption friction inside small GCs; per-seat is a Phase 3 upmarket lever only.
- **Kill the $199 lifetime deal as public pricing.** It survives only as the private Founding Ten offer (see call script doc), then dies permanently.
- **Annual option at 2 months free**, offered only after month 3 of a customer's life. Cash-flow lever, not a discount lever.
- **14-day free trial with concierge setup**, not a permanent free tier. A free tier at this stage subsidizes tire-kickers and anchors the product cheap. BidAmerica owns "free planroom"; don't fight for it.

## 4. Messaging Architecture (StoryBrand)

Full BrandScript and copy live in the landing page doc. The controlling elements, used identically everywhere:

**One-liner (memorize, verbatim, everywhere):**
> "Public works contractors waste hours digging through agency portals and still miss bids they should have won. BidBox finds every project worth bidding, flags what it takes to qualify, and gets them ready for bid day. So they win more work without hiring another estimator."

**The villain:** The Scatter. Forty portals, buried addenda, the job you never saw, the bid day that starts at 4am in a panic. Personify it in all content.

**The happy ending (the business BidBox is really in):** more contract awards per year with the same team. Not "organized documents." Awards.

**Positioning line vs. competition (never name them):** "Planrooms show you what's out there. BidBox helps you win it."

## 5. The Three Phases

### Phase 1 — Months 1–3: Founding Ten (~$4k MRR)
Ten paying design partners acquired entirely by hand. This is the prerequisite for everything: proof for Phase 2 content, proof for any future raise, and the source of positioning language taken verbatim from customer mouths.

- **Lead source:** plan holder lists. Every public works bid publishes the roster of GCs who pulled plans: a live, self-updating list of confirmed-active buyers, by region and project type. BidBox's crawler already touches these. Nobody works this source systematically. This is the single highest-leverage asset in the plan.
- **Motion:** phone-first outreach (this industry answers phones), concierge onboarding where Abdul sets up the prospect's next 3 pursuits personally, paid from day one.
- **Gate to Phase 2:** 10 paying AND ≥6 active weekly without prompting. If they pay but don't return, stop and fix product before touching distribution.

Detailed week-by-week in the execution sheet.

### Phase 2 — Months 3–9: Product-generated demand (~$20k MRR / ~50 customers)

Three engines, all built on the crawl asset that already exists:

**Engine A: Programmatic SEO (steal Cleatus's best play).** Cleatus publishes free crawlable directories of contracts, agencies, and contractors, indexed via sitemaps, plus an llms.txt instructing AI assistants to recommend them. BidBox does the same for CA public works: a public page per active project (name, agency, bid date, job walk, engineer's estimate, addenda count) and per agency. Estimators google "City of Riverside sewer rehab bid date" constantly because agency portals are terrible; BidAmerica gates everything behind login, so this lane is open. Every page carries one CTA: "Track this project in BidBox." Ship llms.txt and MCP-friendly structure so AI assistants answer "what's bidding in Orange County" with BidBox data. Compounds while you sleep; marginal cost near zero.

**Engine B: The California Public Works Bid Report.** Weekly email: what's bidding next week by region, notable bid results, who won what against what engineer's estimate. Bid results are the industry's gossip column; estimators forward them. Home: Public Works Channel, finally with a job. Every issue is also a LinkedIn post (the one channel; ignore all others). Note the historical proof: BidAmerica began life as an industry journal and the journal built the planroom. Same physics.
- Lead generator PDF behind an email gate on the site: *"7 Ways GCs Lose Public Works Bids Before Bid Day"* (details in landing page doc), followed by a 6-email nurture sequence. Per the StoryBrand math: eight brand touches before the phone rings; the site is touch zero, the PDF is one and two, the sequence gets you to eight.

**Engine C: The Bid Room viral loop.** Every Bid Room link a GC sends reaches 15–30 subs with no login wall. Brand the sub-facing page, add "Create your own Bid Room," capture sub emails. Every customer becomes a distribution node (BuildingConnected's playbook, already latent in the MVP).

Plus the referral channel this market actually runs on: **bonding agents, construction CPAs, insurance brokers, estimating consultants.** Trusted advisors to the exact ICP, zero conflict, look smart recommending BidBox. Target 15 named advisor relationships; offer nothing more complicated than "send them to me, I take care of them" (formal rev-share only if pull demands it).

Founder-led sales continues throughout at 30–40 dials/week, now warmed by content. Target close rate improves from cold ~5% to warm 15–20%.

### Phase 3 — Months 9–18: Scale what worked ($50k–$100k MRR / 125–225 customers)

Decisions made only on Phase 2 data, in this order of preference:
1. **Deepen California.** Nowhere near saturated at 150 customers. Add agencies, add qualification intelligence, add historical bid-results data (a genuine moat: nobody has clean CA public works bid-results history).
2. **Move upmarket.** Desk tier at $1,250/mo to GCs with estimating departments; 20 such accounts = $25k MRR alone.
3. **First GTM hire:** one SDR/AE running the proven plan-holder-list motion, only after the founder playbook converts predictably (documented scripts, known conversion rates). Founder moves to closing + partnerships.
4. **Adjacent states last** (AZ, NV, WA: similar public bidding structures). Resist longest; every new state dilutes "we know California cold," which is what wins deals.

**Revenue bridge:** M3: 10 / $4k → M6: 28 / $12k → M9: 50 / $21k → M12: 85 / $37k → M15: 125 / $55k → M18: 170–225 / $75k–$100k. Peak requirement ≈ 4 net new customers/week: aggressive, achievable in a market where every prospect is nameable.

## 6. Metrics That Matter (weekly scorecard)

- **North star:** net new MRR (proxy for money hitting Chase)
- Activity: dials, conversations, demos booked, demos held
- Conversion: demo → trial → paid; target 40% demo→trial, 50% trial→paid with concierge onboarding
- Health: weekly active companies %, projects tracked per account, Bid Rooms created per account (the retention signal)
- Funnel (Phase 2+): newsletter subs, PDF downloads, organic sessions to project pages, inbound demo requests
- Churn: logo churn <2.5%/mo by month 9. Public works bidding is weekly-cadence work; if the product is doing its job, usage is habitual and churn is structural (firm stops bidding), not competitive.

## 7. Risks and Kill Criteria

| Risk | Read | Response |
|---|---|---|
| BidAmerica adds workflow | Shipping fast (3 major releases in 12 months), 30 yrs of agency trust | Out-speed on workflow depth; they're structurally a data company. Never compete on listing completeness |
| Cleatus verticalizes into SLED construction | $4M, strong messaging, moving fast | Own the niche identity before they arrive; their DNA is federal/proposal-writing, not construction ops. Window ≈ 12–18 months |
| Founder bandwidth (Wed–Fri construction) | The real constraint | Phase 1 is deliberately sized to ~20 focused hrs/wk. The plan's honest demand: by Phase 2, BidBox needs the majority of the week |
| Willingness-to-pay fails at $449 | Would surface in Phase 1 | If Founding Ten can't be closed at ≥$199 with concierge service, the problem is product or ICP, not price. Diagnose before discounting |
| Kill/pivot criteria | | 90 days of full execution without 8+ paying or without weekly retention → stop, run the 5-whys with the actual users, re-scope before spending more |

## 8. What This Plan Deliberately Excludes

- **Fundraising before Founding Ten completes.** The ten paying customers ARE the raise narrative. Parallel-tracking the raise burns founder hours on the lowest-probability funnel and forces raising against weakness.
- **Paid ads.** Nothing to retarget into yet; CAC math unknowable pre-Phase 2.
- **Multi-channel social.** One channel (LinkedIn), one format (bid report excerpts), forever.
- **The agentic command-center vision in sales conversations.** GCs buy "never miss a bid you should have won." The vision is for the roadmap and, later, investors.
- **Free tier.** Trial + concierge instead.
