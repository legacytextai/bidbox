# BidBox Founding Customer Call Script + Offer Terms

No em dashes anywhere in this document. It is external-facing language.

---

## Pre-call prep (90 seconds per prospect, non-negotiable)

Before dialing, know three things from the plan holder list data:
1. The specific project they pulled plans on (name, agency, bid date)
2. Whether they bid it, and if results are out, how they placed
3. One other live project in their county that matches their profile

The entire cold call works because you open with #1. Without it, do not dial.

---

## The cold call

**Opener (10 seconds, earn 30 more):**

> "Hey [Name], this is Abdul. You guys pulled plans on the [project name] job for [agency] last month, right? I'm calling about that world. Do you have 30 seconds? If it's useless you can hang up on me."

Notes: naming their actual bid instantly separates you from every telemarketer. The permission ask lowers defenses. Do not say "how are you today."

**If they give you the 30 seconds, the one-liner plus credibility (verbatim):**

> "I spent years running ops at a public works GC here in SoCal, so I know the drill. You're checking a dozen agency sites, digging through specs, and hoping you don't miss an addendum. I built BidBox to kill that. It finds every public works job you should be bidding, pulls the plans and specs, flags what it takes to qualify, and gets you ready for bid day. Basically it does the grunt work so you win more jobs without hiring another estimator."

**Then immediately to discovery (pick 2 or 3, then stop talking):**

1. "Walk me through it. When a bid like [project] comes out, who finds it and how?"
2. "How many jobs a month are you actually bidding versus how many you wish you could get to?"
3. "When's the last time an addendum or a job walk requirement burned you?"
4. "What does the morning of bid day look like at your shop?"
5. "What are you using today? Portals and email? Anything else?"

Rules: mine for the pain in their words. Write down their exact phrases. Do not pitch features during discovery. When they describe a pain, agitate once ("So that job just slipped by and you found out when results posted?") then move to the ask.

**The ask:**

> "Here's all I want. Give me 15 minutes on a screen share this week. I'll have your counties and your agencies already loaded with live jobs, so you're looking at your world, not a canned demo. If it's not obviously useful in 15 minutes, we're done and I won't chase you. Does Thursday morning or afternoon work better?"

Always offer two times. Book it on the call; do not send a link and hope.

## Objection handlers

**"We already use BidAmerica / a planroom / Dodge."**
> "Good, keep it if you like it. That tells you what's out there. BidBox is what happens next: it reads the specs, flags bond and license and DIR requirements, tracks every addendum, and runs your bid day. The planroom is the phone book. This is the part that wins the job. Worth 15 minutes to see the difference on your own projects?"

**"We're too busy right now."**
> "That's exactly the problem I'm selling against. Busy is why bids slip. Here's what I'll do: before we even talk again, I'll set up your next three pursuits in BidBox myself. You show up to a 15-minute call and everything's already tracked. Zero work on your end. Fair?"

**"How much is it?"** (early in the call)
> "For the first ten companies I'm working with directly, less than what one missed addendum costs you. Real answer when I show you the thing, because the price only makes sense next to what it does. 15 minutes Thursday?"

**"Send me some information."**
> "I could, but a PDF won't show you your own projects. Tell you what, I'll send a two-minute video of BidBox loaded with [their county] jobs plus one page on how it works. If it looks interesting, we do 15 minutes. Deal?"
(Then actually send a screen recording of their county. This converts.)

**"We're a small shop, we don't need software."**
> "Small shops are exactly who this is for. The big GCs have three estimators and a Procore license. You've got yourself and a pile of portals. BidBox is how a three-person team bids like a fifteen-person team."

**"Is this AI stuff even accurate?"**
> "Fair question, and the honest answer is: trust but verify, same as a junior estimator. Every date, bond requirement, and addendum links straight back to the source document, so you check it in one click instead of reading 400 pages. And during founding onboarding I'm personally reviewing your projects with you."

## The demo call (15 minutes, their data)

1. **(1 min) Frame:** "Goal today: you tell me if this saves you real hours. I loaded [their agencies]. Stop me anytime."
2. **(6 min) Show the pipeline on their world:** live projects in their counties → one project opened → extracted deadline, job walk, bond, engineer's estimate, addenda count → the calendar view → the alert that would have fired.
3. **(3 min) Bid Room:** create one live for a real project. "This link goes to your subs. No login, no account. They see docs and drop quotes. On bid day you export the whole thing to Excel."
4. **(2 min) Tie back to their discovery pain, verbatim:** "You said [their exact phrase]. This is the piece that kills that."
5. **(3 min) The founding offer and the close.** Read the terms below, then: "I'm capping this at ten companies because I'm personally onboarding every one. Want one of the slots?"

Then be quiet. First person to talk loses.

**If yes:** send the Stripe link while still on the call, book the 45-minute onboarding session before hanging up.
**If maybe:** "What would need to be true for this to be a yes?" Handle it or disqualify. Offer a 14-day trial with concierge setup as the fallback, with a booked end-of-trial call on the calendar before hanging up.
