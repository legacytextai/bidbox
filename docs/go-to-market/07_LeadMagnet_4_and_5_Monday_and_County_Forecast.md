# The 30-Minute Monday
### The weekly routine that replaces portal-checking forever

*Design direction: 4 pages, workbook feel, with a fill-in schedule block. This is the "process" magnet for the operator-minded buyer. It also quietly demonstrates that the manual version of this routine is painful, which is the sale. No em dashes.*

## Cover
**THE 30-MINUTE MONDAY**
How disciplined GCs cover every public works opportunity in their counties in one sitting a week.

## Page 1: Why Monday
Bid discovery fails not from laziness but from formlessness. "Check the portals when I get a chance" means never, because chances don't come. The fix is a fixed appointment: same time, same list, same order, every Monday, 30 minutes, phone off.

What 30 disciplined minutes replaces: the scattered 3 to 4 hours most estimators actually spend across the week, plus the jobs that slip through anyway.

## Page 2: The Routine
**Minutes 0–15: Sweep.**
Work a fixed source list in a fixed order (your county public works pages, the school districts in your area, special districts, and any planroom you subscribe to). For each new posting, capture only four fields into your pipeline sheet: project name, agency, bid date, one-line scope. Do not read specs during the sweep. Sweeping and estimating are different modes; mixing them is how 30 minutes becomes 3 hours.

**Minutes 15–25: Screen.**
Run each new capture through the six-point go/no-go (license match, DIR, bond capacity, prequal deadline, mandatory walk, experience clause). Kill fast. A dead pursuit killed on Monday costs 10 minutes; killed on bid week it costs 40 hours.

**Minutes 25–30: Schedule.**
Every survivor gets three calendar entries before you stand up: bid date, job walk, and a mid-window addenda check. If it's not calendared, it doesn't exist.

## Page 3: The Source List (fill-in worksheet)
A table with 15 blank rows: Source / URL / Posting cadence / Last checked. Pre-filled examples: county PW page, two city portals, a school district, a water district, a planroom.

Sidebar: **The honest problem with this routine.** It only covers the sources on your list. California has hundreds of posting agencies, and the job that would have been perfect for you has a habit of appearing on the one portal you don't check. The routine shrinks the leak; it can't close it.

## Template structure

**Cover:** THE ORANGE COUNTY PUBLIC WORKS BID FORECAST | July 14 – August 14, 2026 | [N] active opportunities | Compiled by BidBox from [M] agency sources.

**Page 1: The Table.** One row per project: Project name / Agency / Bid date / Job walk (date, mandatory flag) / Engineer's estimate (where published) / License class / One-line scope. Sorted by bid date. Rows with job walks inside 7 days highlighted.

**Page 2: The Watch List.** Projects announced but not yet out to bid (agenda items, board approvals, pre-solicitation notices). This section is the differentiator; nobody hands GCs the forward look.

**Fixed footer copy on every page:**
> This forecast is a snapshot. Addenda, date changes, and new postings happen daily. BidBox tracks all of it live and alerts you the moment anything in your pipeline moves. Free trial at bidbox.app.

**Fixed intro paragraph:**
> Every public works project bidding in [County] over the next 30 days, compiled from [M] city, county, school district, and special district sources, so nothing in your backyard slips past you. Forward this to anyone who bids public work; they'll owe you one.

**Production notes:**
- Generated weekly per target county (start: Orange, LA, Riverside, San Bernardino, San Diego)
- Must carry a visible "generated [date]" stamp; a stale forecast damages trust more than no forecast
- The "forward this" line is the built-in viral mechanic; the forward recipient hits the gate to get their own county
- Accuracy bar is absolute: one wrong bid date in this document costs more credibility than the document earns. QA before every send until the pipeline has proven itself for a month
