Hundreds of California agencies, each posting bids on its own website, its own format, its own quirks. Addenda on one page, plans behind a planholder form, the job walk buried in paragraph four of a PDF notice.

## EMAIL 3 — Day 4 — Value (the routine)
**Subject: The 30-minute Monday**

[First name],

The best estimating desks I've seen all run some version of the same ritual. I call it the 30-Minute Monday. Same time every week, phone off:

Minutes 0–15: Sweep. A fixed list of agency sources, checked in a fixed order. New postings get captured with four fields only: name, agency, bid date, one-line scope. No spec reading during the sweep.

Minutes 15–25: Screen. Each capture runs the six-point go/no-go: license match, DIR, bond capacity, prequal deadline, mandatory walk, experience clause. Kill fast. A pursuit killed Monday costs 10 minutes. Killed on bid week, it costs 40 hours.

Minutes 25–30: Schedule. Every survivor gets three calendar entries: bid date, job walk, mid-window addenda check. Not calendared, doesn't exist.

Run this for a month and you will feel the difference on bid days.

Full honesty about its limit: the routine only covers the sources on your list, and California has hundreds of posting agencies. This is exactly the gap BidBox was built to close. It runs the sweep across every agency daily, extracts the six go/no-go items from the documents automatically, and calendars the deadlines for you. The Monday half hour goes back to pricing work instead of hunting for it.

Either way, steal the routine. It's yours.

Abdul
