## Intro (page 2)

Most public works bids are not lost on price.

They are lost days or weeks earlier, quietly, to a detail buried in a 400-page spec book or a portal nobody checked on the right morning. The low bidder did not out-build you. They out-organized you.

Here are the seven ways it happens, pulled from years of running bids at a California public works GC. Each one ends with the fix. Print the checklist on the last page and pin it above the estimating desk.

---

## 1. The job you never saw

California public agencies post bids across hundreds of separate websites: cities, counties, school districts, water districts, transit agencies. No GC checks them all. Every month, jobs that fit your exact profile bid and award without you ever knowing they existed.

**The math that should bother you:** if you bid 8 jobs a month and win 1 in 6, every 6 missed opportunities is a missed award. Not a missed bid. A missed contract.

**The fix:** one system of record for discovery. Whether that is a planroom subscription, a paid service, or an intern with a checklist, someone owns "nothing in our counties gets posted without us seeing it within 24 hours." Unowned means unchecked.

## 2. The addendum that disqualified you

Addenda drop late, change everything, and get buried. A revised bid form, a new bond percentage, a changed line item. Submit on the old form and your bid is non-responsive. You will find out at bid opening, in public, after doing all the work.

**The tell:** if your addenda process is "we check the portal again a day or two before bid," you are exposed. Agencies routinely issue Addendum 3 within 72 hours of opening.

**The fix:** every active pursuit gets monitored for addenda daily, automatically or by name-assigned habit. Every addendum gets a two-line log entry: what changed, what we did about it. Acknowledgment boxes get checked the day the addendum drops, not on bid day.

## 3. The mandatory job walk nobody attended

Two words in the notice, "attendance mandatory," and missing the walk ends the pursuit before it starts. Job walks get scheduled with short notice, at inconvenient hours, at sites two counties away. The estimator finds out the walk was Tuesday when it is Thursday.

**The fix:** job walk status is a field, not a footnote. The moment a project enters your pipeline, three things are extracted and calendared: is there a walk, is it mandatory, when and where. If it is mandatory and unattended, the project is dead; stop spending hours on it.

## 4. The qualification detail found too late

License classification. DIR registration. Bond percentage. Prequalification packets due before the bid. Experience requirements ("three similar projects in the last five years"). Any one of these, discovered on day 12 of a 14-day bid window, either kills the pursuit or forces a scramble that produces a sloppy number.

**The fix:** qualify in the first hour, not the last day. A one-page go/no-go screen, run the day a project enters the pipeline: license class, DIR, bond capacity, experience requirements, prequal deadline. Ten minutes up front saves forty hours of dead estimating work.

## 5. The sub quotes that never came

You emailed twelve subs a link to a 300 MB folder, or worse, asked them to make an account on a portal. Half never opened it. Three said they would price it and went quiet. On bid day you are missing two trades and plugging numbers you do not believe in.

**The truth about subs:** they are bidding for ten GCs at once. The GC that is easiest to price for gets the coverage. Every login wall, every giant download, every unclear scope costs you quotes.

**The fix:** frictionless sub access (one link, no accounts, documents organized by trade) plus a coverage board: every scope listed, every sub invited, status per sub. The Thursday-before-bid question is never "who do we think is pricing this," it is a list with holes you can see and chase.

## 6. The bid day scramble

Quotes across nine email threads, two voicemails, and a text. The bid form half-filled. Somebody re-checking the addenda count at 6am. Bid day chaos does not just feel bad, it produces worse numbers: rushed plugs, transcription errors, and no time to shop the last two scopes.

**The fix:** bid day is an assembly, not an event. The rule: by 48 hours out, everything lives in one place, one spreadsheet, one owner. The last morning is for sharpening the number, not for finding it.

## 7. Nobody learned anything

The bid results post. You were 4% high. The file gets closed and the lesson evaporates. Six months later you bid the same agency, against the same competitors, blind again. The most expensive data your company produces, its own bid history against public results, sits unused.

**The fix:** a fifteen-minute post-bid ritual for every result: who won, at what number, spread to your bid, spread to the engineer's estimate, one sentence on why. Twelve months of that and you will know which agencies you win, which competitors to respect, and which jobs to stop bidding entirely.

## The Pre-Bid Checklist (final page, printable)

**The day it enters the pipeline:**
- [ ] Bid date, time, and delivery method calendared
- [ ] Job walk: exists / mandatory / date calendared
- [ ] License class and DIR registration confirmed
- [ ] Bond requirement vs. current capacity confirmed
- [ ] Prequalification requirement and deadline checked
- [ ] Go/no-go decision made and logged

**Every day it is active:**
- [ ] Addenda checked; new addenda logged and acknowledged

**One week out:**
- [ ] Sub coverage board reviewed; open scopes chased
- [ ] All quotes flowing to one place

**48 hours out:**
- [ ] Bid form current per latest addendum
- [ ] All addenda acknowledged on the form
- [ ] One owner, one spreadsheet, one final number process

**After results:**
- [ ] Result logged: winner, number, spreads, one-sentence lesson
